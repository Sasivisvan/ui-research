# Craft Distinctive Websites From Sixty Proven Parts

A professional, non-generic website in late 2026 is built from about sixty standard components: header, menus, hero, cards, carousel, modal, toast, form controls, loaders, cursor, footer and the rest. It reads as crafted, and not as "AI slop", only when one concept drives every choice: one committed typeface, real imagery, specific copy, one signature interaction, and a small motion system applied the same way everywhere. The generic look is a recognisable bundle. It has Inter or Roboto type, a purple-to-indigo gradient on white, a centred hero with two pill buttons, three icon cards, a uniform 16px radius, and the same fade-up on every section ([prg.sh](https://prg.sh/ramblings/Why-Your-AI-Keeps-Building-the-Same-Purple-Gradient-Website); [925 Studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-web-design-guide)). Each component below has a generic default and several crafted variants you can trace to Codrops, Olivier Larose, Aceternity, Magic UI and studios such as Lusion. The platform now does much of the hard work natively. `<dialog>`, Popover, invoker commands, anchor positioning, `@starting-style` and View Transitions are all usable, and scroll-driven animations reached all three engines when Firefox 159 shipped on **27 October 2026** ([DEV](https://dev.to/richicodes/scroll-driven-animations-are-baseline-at-last-3c8g)). GSAP is **100% free, including SplitText and MorphSVG** ([GSAP 3.13](https://gsap.com/blog/3-13/)). Lenis is the default smooth-scroll library, and Base UI became shadcn/ui's default primitive layer in July 2026 ([shadcn changelog](https://ui.shadcn.com/docs/changelog/2026-07-base-ui-default)). Licensing is the most common hidden trap. "Free" rarely means CC0: Font Awesome Free icons are CC BY 4.0, Remix Icon changed its licence in January 2026, Pangram Pangram fonts are trial-only, Reshot has retired, and Coverr now bans AI training. Finally, all of it has to hold up on a mid-range phone: **LCP ≤ 2.5 s, INP ≤ 200 ms, CLS ≤ 0.1 at the 75th percentile** ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/vitals)), hover effects gated to fine pointers, 44px touch targets, `svh` units and a reduced-motion path for every effect.

## Crafted sites run on one concept, one typeface and one motion system

The generic look is easy to spot. It shows purple or indigo accents and gradients on white, which trace back to Tailwind's old `bg-indigo-500` default (Adam Wathan has apologised for it). It uses Inter, Roboto or Arial, a centred hero with a CTA, a three-box icon grid, rounded corners everywhere and faint shadows. It also has functional tells: no validation, no error states and weak keyboard support ([prg.sh](https://prg.sh/ramblings/Why-Your-AI-Keeps-Building-the-Same-Purple-Gradient-Website)). Studio critics add more tells. The same 16px radius, 24px padding and card height appear on every element. Illustrations look "too smooth, too symmetrical, plastic". Headlines are vague ("Build the future of work"), hover states do nothing, and the same fade-in plays on every block ([925 Studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-web-design-guide)). The escape route has its own clichés. Agents asked to look "less AI" tend to produce warm cream with a terracotta accent, or near-black with one acid-green highlight. Even with anti-slop prompts, models fall back on second-tier defaults such as Space Grotesk ([prg.sh](https://prg.sh/ramblings/Why-Your-AI-Keeps-Building-the-Same-Purple-Gradient-Website)). None of these choices is wrong in isolation. The problem is the unexamined default bundle. These sources are mostly studio and vendor blogs, and no rigorous study measures how common the pattern is. Treat the tells as a practitioner consensus, not a measured fact.

The antidote is to settle a concept before choosing any component. Write one sentence describing what the brand is like ("a field notebook", "a 1970s ski lodge"), then let it decide type, grid, colour, motion and copy. Concrete remedies follow from that. Pick **one distinctive typeface and use it decisively**, with extreme weight contrast (100–200 against 800–900) and size jumps of 3× or more. Choose a dominant colour with sharp accents rather than a timid, evenly spread palette, and name tokens by role (`--color-action-primary`, not `--color-gradient-start`). Break the centred hero with asymmetric or split layouts, and vary radius and padding on purpose to show hierarchy ([prg.sh](https://prg.sh/ramblings/Why-Your-AI-Keeps-Building-the-Same-Purple-Gradient-Website)). Use real product screenshots and team photos. Write copy that states what the product does, as Stripe's "Financial infrastructure for the internet" does, and test each line with "would the CEO actually say it?" ([925 Studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-web-design-guide)). Editing matters as much as adding. Immersive Garden says it "spent more time removing ideas than creating new ones" ([CommArts](https://www.commarts.com/webpicks/immersive-garden)). A practical workflow is to collect 3–5 references, write down *why* each works, ask for several design directions, and then refine one dimension at a time ([prg.sh](https://prg.sh/ramblings/Why-Your-AI-Keeps-Building-the-Same-Purple-Gradient-Website)).

### Typography has a half-life: yesterday's escape fonts are today's defaults

Google Fonts' own metadata measures saturation. Of **1,959 families**, the most used are Roboto (#2), Open Sans (#3), Inter (#5), Montserrat (#7) and Poppins (#8). The 2024–25 "distinctive" picks have since gone mainstream: Space Grotesk is #58, Bricolage Grotesque #60, Instrument Serif #79 and Geist #85 ([Google Fonts metadata](https://fonts.google.com/metadata/fonts)). Serif headlines are also turning generic now that AI companies use them to "look more human" ([Wired via AI Directory](https://aidirectory.com/news/ai-companies-turn-to-serif-fonts-to-look-more-human)). Good licence-safe faces that are less saturated include Young Serif (#490), Gloock (#393), Familjen Grotesk (#442), Host Grotesk (#394), Funnel Display, Martian Mono (#572), Fragment Mono, Big Shoulders, Science Gothic and Zalando Sans Expanded ([Google Fonts metadata](https://fonts.google.com/metadata/fonts)). Fontshare's ITF families (Zodiak, Gambetta, Boska, Clash, Cabinet Grotesk, Panchang, Tanker) are free for commercial use ([Fontshare API](https://api.fontshare.com/v2/fonts?limit=200)). Satoshi and Switzer are becoming common, though, so they are drifting toward default status as well. A popularity rank above about 150 works as a rough proxy for "not overused". Limit a site to two families plus an optional mono, and use variable axes (opsz, wdth, SOFT/WONK, CASL) to get character out of a single file.

| Register | Display face | Text / UI face | Why it avoids the default |
|---|---|---|---|
| Editorial | Newsreader (opsz) or Young Serif; Zodiak (Fontshare) | Literata or Source Serif 4; Familjen Grotesk for UI | Optical sizes and real italics; not the Instrument Serif + Inter cliché |
| Brutalist / raw | Big Shoulders or Anybody (wdth); Collletttivo Sinistre | Martian Mono or Fragment Mono | Width axes and mono labels read as intentional |
| Tech / developer | Mona Sans or Hubot Sans (wdth); Science Gothic | Martian Mono, Recursive, Azeret Mono | Avoids the now-ubiquitous Geist + Geist Mono |
| Luxury / fashion | Bodoni Moda (opsz), Gloock; Boska (Fontshare) | Schibsted Grotesk or Switzer | High contrast display with tracked small caps |
| Playful / consumer | Bricolage Grotesque tuned off defaults; Funnel Display | Onest or Rethink Sans | Expressive but body-legible |

These pairings are research-team recommendations built from licence-verified OFL and ITF FFL families. They are a starting point, not a cited rule.

### A motion system, not a pile of effects

The craft experts agree closely on motion. Emil Kowalski (Sonner, Vaul) keeps UI animation **under 300 ms**, uses ease-out for snappy feedback, and never uses `ease-in` for UI. He animates only `transform` and `opacity`, and prefers CSS transitions because they can be interrupted and retargeted mid-flight. Buttons press to `scale(0.97)`. Entrances never start from `scale(0)`: start from 0.93 or higher. Popovers scale from their trigger, not from the centre. A 180 ms select feels more responsive than a 400 ms one, and a 2px blur can hide a crossfade seam ([Emil Kowalski](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/7-practical-animation-tips); [Great animations](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/great-animations)). Rauno Freiberg adds that frequency should decide whether something animates at all. Removing motion from a command menu he used hundreds of times a day made him "feel like I was moving much faster". He also argues that destructive gestures should commit only on release ([Rauno](https://rauno.me/craft/interaction-design)). Vercel's guidelines codify the rest: never `transition: all`, animate only when it clarifies cause and effect, make motion cancellable, anchor it to where it physically starts, give spinners a 150–300 ms show-delay and a 300–500 ms minimum display, and always provide a reduced-motion variant ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/design/guidelines)). Material 3 publishes the most concrete token set: durations from 50 to 1000 ms in sixteen steps, standard easing `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1)`, and spring presets that are slightly underdamped for position and critically damped for colour ([Material Motion.md](https://github.com/material-components/material-components-android/blob/master/docs/theming/Motion.md)). Timing should be asymmetric. In the Next.js view-transition guide, old content leaves in 150 ms while new content fades in over 210 ms after a 150 ms delay and slides over 400 ms, and a 60px offset is enough to show direction ([Next.js](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/guides/view-transitions)). Josh Comeau's test for the token set: if custom curves creep past about 20% of your animations, the tokens need work ([Comeau](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/animation/linear-timing-function/)).

```css
/* One token set for the whole site (tune values; regenerate linear() springs with a generator) */
:root {
  color-scheme: light dark;
  --brand-h: 32;                                   /* pick the hue from the concept */
  --brand-500: oklch(62% 0.16 var(--brand-h));
  --surface: light-dark(oklch(99% .005 80), oklch(16% .01 80));   /* tinted, never pure */
  --text:    light-dark(oklch(16% .01 80), oklch(94% .01 80));
  --action:  light-dark(oklch(45% .13 var(--brand-h)), var(--brand-500));
  --step-0: clamp(1.125rem, 1.08rem + .23vw, 1.25rem);           /* Utopia-style fluid type, keeps rem for zoom */
  --step-5: clamp(2.8rem, 2.2rem + 3vw, 4.8rem);
  --radius-s: 4px; --radius-m: 10px; --radius-pill: 999px;       /* deliberate, not 16px everywhere */
  --dur-instant: 80ms; --dur-fast: 150ms; --dur-base: 220ms; --dur-slow: 350ms; --dur-page: 450ms;
  --ease-out: cubic-bezier(.22,1,.36,1);       /* easeOutQuint: UI default */
  --ease-out-expo: cubic-bezier(.16,1,.3,1);   /* image reveals, page moves */
  --ease-in-out: cubic-bezier(.65,0,.35,1);    /* things that move while staying on screen */
  --ease-exit: cubic-bezier(.3,0,.8,.15);      /* M3 emphasized accelerate: exits only */
  --spring: cubic-bezier(.34,1.56,.64,1);      /* fallback; override with linear() under @supports */
}
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference) {   /* no-motion-first authoring */
  .btn { transition: transform var(--dur-instant) var(--ease-out), background-color var(--dur-fast) var(--ease-out); }
  .btn:active { transform: scale(.97); }
}
```

Comeau recommends writing CSS no-motion-first, as above, so browsers that don't understand the media query also get no motion. Opacity fades are "safe to use for everyone" ([Comeau](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/react/prefers-reduced-motion/)). Springs built with `linear()` need 40–50 or more points to look natural, and three spring strings add only about 1.3 kB gzipped ([Comeau](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/animation/linear-timing-function/)).

## Sixty components, each with a default to avoid and variants worth building

Use **headless, accessibility-first primitives for behaviour** and **effect libraries for ideas only**. Base UI 1.x (MUI, about 35 accessible components, now `@base-ui/react`) is shadcn/ui's default for new projects since July 2026, and `shadcn init -b radix` keeps Radix ([shadcn](https://ui.shadcn.com/docs/changelog/2026-07-base-ui-default); [InfoQ](https://infoq.com/news/2026/02/baseui-v1-accessible/)). React Aria leads on internationalisation and date/time, with 30+ languages, 13 calendar systems and segmented date fields ([React Aria](https://react-aria.adobe.com/)). Ark UI offers 45+ state-machine components for React, Solid, Vue and Svelte ([Ark UI](https://ark-ui.com/)). The effect catalogues share one vocabulary: shimmer sweeps, border beams, magnetic pull, text scramble, spotlight and neubrutalism. That vocabulary is the 2026 template look. The catalogues are Aceternity (about 262 components via the shadcn CLI, though counts conflict, [21st.dev](https://21st.dev/@manuarora700/library/aceternity-ui)), Magic UI ([docs](https://magicui.design/docs/components)), Cult UI ([docs](https://www.cult-ui.com/docs)), Animata ([docs](https://animata.design/docs)) and Hover.dev ([buttons](https://www.hover.dev/components/buttons)). Use them as style references, change their easing, colour and scale, and rebuild them on accessible primitives. Most ship without reduced-motion handling or ARIA state. For component-specific references, use Navbar Gallery ([navbar.gallery](https://www.navbar.gallery/)), footer.design ([footer.design](https://www.footer.design/)), Supahero for heroes ([supahero.io](https://supahero.io/)) and the Codrops GitHub org, which hosts dozens of MIT-licensed menu and hover demos ([github.com/codrops](https://github.com/Codrops)).

### Navigation and page structure: show the links, move with spatial origin

NN/g's hamburger study (179 participants) found that hidden navigation was used **27% of the time on desktop versus 48% when visible**. Discoverability dropped by more than 20%, and tasks took at least 39% longer on desktop. NN/g's advice is to avoid hidden navigation on desktop, and on mobile to show the top-level links when there are four or fewer ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/hamburger-menus/)). Sticky headers should earn their place. Use opaque rather than translucent backgrounds, and a partially persistent reveal that slides in over 300–400 ms on a small upward scroll ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/sticky-headers/)). For mega menus, NN/g recommends about a 0.5 s hover delay and a 0.5 s grace period on leave. Radix ships with a 200 ms open delay and a 300 ms skip delay ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mega-menus-work-well/); [Radix](https://www.radix-ui.com/primitives/docs/components/navigation-menu)). In production, 150–300 ms plus a "safe triangle" for diagonal pointer moves is the practical middle ground. For semantics, site navigation is **links in a labelled `<nav>`, with disclosure buttons for submenus, never `role="menu"`** ([APG](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/disclosure/examples/disclosure-navigation/)). Award-style menu motion comes from a small set of Codrops/GSAP techniques: clip-path reveals from the button, the 2026 EaseReverseClipMenu with its "smoother, snappier" reversed easing ([repo](https://github.com/codrops/EaseReverseClipMenu)), marquee rows, letter shuffles, and MenuToGrid's GSAP Flip morph ([Codrops org](https://github.com/Codrops)). Headers that change state on scroll now work in CSS. `scroll-state(stuck)` arrived in Chrome 133, and `scroll-state(scrolled)` for hide-on-scroll is rolling out in Chrome 144. Both are Chromium-only, so ship a JS fallback ([una.im](https://una.im/scroll-state-scrolled); [Chrome](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/css-scroll-state-queries)).

```css
/* Hide-on-scroll header: CSS where supported (Chromium), rAF-throttled passive scroll listener elsewhere */
html { container-type: scroll-state; scroll-padding-top: var(--header-h, 72px); }
.site-header { position: sticky; top: 0; background: var(--surface); transition: translate .3s var(--ease-out); }
@container scroll-state(scrolled: bottom) { .site-header { translate: 0 -100%; } }
@container scroll-state(scrolled: top)    { .site-header { translate: 0 0; } }
.site-header:focus-within, .site-header:has([aria-expanded="true"]) { translate: 0 0 !important; }
```

### Actions and inputs: research beats fashion on forms

Form research is unusually consistent. **Use persistent labels outside the field.** NN/g found that placeholder-as-label strains memory and hinders error fixing, and that floating labels are "better than label-as-placeholder but worse than an outside label" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/form-design-placeholders/)). Baymard saw "severe flow and typing issues" with inline labels on mobile ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/mobile-forms-avoid-inline-labels)), and Adam Silver lists a dozen ways float labels fail ([adamsilver.io](https://adamsilver.io/articles/float-labels-are-problematic/)). On validation, **31% of benchmarked e-commerce sites give no inline validation**. Validate on blur, check fixed-length fields once complete, clear errors as soon as they are fixed, and show positive validation ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/inline-form-validation)). Smashing's "reward early, punish late" rule adds that empty required fields should be flagged only on submit ([Smashing](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2022/09/inline-validation-web-forms-ux/)). GOV.UK's date pattern uses three `inputmode="numeric"` fields, accepts month names (which "reduced errors dramatically"), and **never auto-advances focus** ([GOV.UK](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/date-input/)). Toggles must take effect immediately ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/toggle-switch-guidelines/)). Replacing a search link with a visible box raised search use by **91%** ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/search-visible-and-simple/)). The platform caught up in 2026. `field-sizing: content` became Baseline in June 2026 ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/baseline-digest-jun-2026)), `:user-invalid` has been widely available since 2023 ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/Selectors/:user-invalid)), and invoker commands reach about 87% of users ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/mdn-html_elements_button_command)). The customisable `<select>` (`appearance: base-select`) works in Chrome 135+ and Safari 27 but **not Firefox**, about 74% globally, and it removes the native mobile picker ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/customizable-select); [Chrome](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/a-customizable-select)). The Liquid Glass look uses an SVG displacement filter inside `backdrop-filter`, which is **Chromium-only** and GPU-heavy, so reserve it for one or two floating CTAs ([LogRocket](https://blog.logrocket.com/how-create-liquid-glass-effects-css-and-svg/)).

### Content and sections: one bold idea per section, proven by evidence

Award-winning heroes win with **one idea**: oversized or kinetic type, real photography or product, or an object the visitor can touch. Awwwards tags its hero collection by technique (Typography, 3D, Video, Parallax, Fullscreen) ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/websites/hero-section/)), and current inspiration on recent.design, formerly Godly, leans toward tactile experiments such as terrain sculpting and physical textures ([recent.design](http://recent.design/?ref=godly)). The carousel evidence is blunt. NN/g found that a single static hero often beats a carousel, eyetracking showed animated content looked at only **27%** of the time, and carousels should stay at **five frames or fewer**, with a peek of the next slide and no auto-advance on mobile ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/designing-effective-carousels/); [mobile](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-carousels/)). Baymard reports that **46% of homepage carousels have UX performance issues**, and that static sections perform as well as a well-built carousel ([Baymard](https://www.baymard.com/blog/homepage-carousel)). For libraries, Embla scores **100%** on closed accessibility issues, Swiper 86.4%, Splide 53.8% and Owl 27.6% ([Carousa11y](https://cogapplabs.github.io/carousa11y/research)). Chrome 135+ adds native CSS carousels (`::scroll-button`, `::scroll-marker`), which degrade to a plain swipe scroller elsewhere ([Chrome](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/carousels-with-css)). Native masonry (`display: grid-lanes`) shipped only in **Safari 26.4**, so treat it as an enhancement over a normal grid ([web-features](https://web-platform-dx.github.io/web-features-explorer/features/grid-lanes/)). On social proof, purchase likelihood peaks at **4.2–4.5 stars** and falls as ratings approach 5.0 ([Spiegel/PowerReviews via Retail TouchPoints](https://www.retailtouchpoints.com/topics/customer-experience/purchase-likelihood-highest-for-products-rated-between-4-2-and-4-5-out-of-5-stars)), so honest, specific testimonials beat a perfect wall of fives.

### Feedback and overlays: the native stack replaces most libraries

NN/g's rule for modals is to **interrupt only when it's worth the cost**: critical warnings, critical missing information, wizard steps, or one high-value question. Never use a modal for newsletters, checkout or complex decisions ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/modal-nonmodal-dialog/)). For loading, show nothing **under 1 s**, a skeleton for full-page loads or a spinner for a single module **under 10 s**, and a progress bar with a time estimate **beyond 10 s**. Avoid frame-only skeletons ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/skeleton-screens/)). Empty states must communicate status, teach, and offer a direct action, and they must never flash "No records" while data is still loading ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/empty-state-interface-design/)). Emil Kowalski's Sonner and Vaul are the reference builds for crafted overlay motion. Sonner uses interruptible CSS transitions instead of keyframes, stacks toasts at `translateY(-14px) scale(0.95)` per level with matched heights, and dismisses by momentum when velocity exceeds **0.11** with pointer capture. It pauses timers on hover and while the tab is hidden, and Alt+T focuses the toast region ([toast](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/building-a-toast-component); [Sonner docs](https://sonner.emilkowal.ski/toaster)). Vaul writes transforms directly instead of through CSS variables, which kept long lists from lagging. It blocks drag for 100 ms after an inner scroll reaches the top, scales the background page like an iOS sheet stack, uses the iOS easing **`cubic-bezier(0.32, 0.72, 0, 1)` over 500 ms**, and sizes itself above the keyboard from `visualViewport` ([drawer](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/building-a-drawer-component)). The researcher recalls Vaul being marked unmaintained around 2025 but could not confirm it, so check the repo before adopting. Natively, invoker commands (`commandfor`/`command`) have been Baseline since Safari 26.2 in December 2025 ([InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/01/html-invoker-commands)). Anchor positioning became Baseline Newly available with Firefox 147 in January 2026 ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/web-platform-01-2026)), though caniuse still marks Chromium and Firefox as partial, so keep a fallback ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/css-anchor-positioning)). `@starting-style` has been Baseline since 2024 ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@starting-style)). `<dialog closedby>` has **no Safari support**, and the npm `dialog-closedby-polyfill` appeared in Socket's "Shai-Hulud" compromised-package analysis, so audit it or write a five-line fallback ([web-features](https://web-platform-dx.github.io/web-features-explorer/features/dialog-closedby); [Socket](https://socket.dev/npm/package/dialog-closedby-polyfill)).

<dialog id="confirm" role="alertdialog" aria-labelledby="t" closedby="any">
  <h2 id="t">Delete “Atlas”?</h2><p>This permanently removes 128 files.</p>
  <form method="dialog"><button value="cancel" autofocus>Cancel</button><button value="delete">Delete</button></form>
</dialog>
<style>
dialog { opacity:0; translate:0 12px; scale:.98;
  transition: opacity .2s, translate .2s, scale .2s, overlay .2s allow-discrete, display .2s allow-discrete; }

@media (max-width:640px) {   /* same element becomes a bottom sheet */
  dialog { margin:auto 0 0; width:100%; max-width:none; max-height:90dvh; border-radius:20px 20px 0 0;

<script>/* Safari lacks closedby: backdrop click closes */

### Signature components: where the brand gets its one memorable moment

These are the components that award sites use for personality, and the ones most likely to hurt performance or accessibility. Custom cursors are a decorative layer above the real pointer. Olivier Larose's blend-mode tutorial uses refs, a rAF loop with `lerp(…, 0.075)`, `mix-blend-mode: difference`, `pointer-events: none`, and a hover state that grows from 30 to 400px with a blur. It does not handle touch at all, so production code must add gating ([Larose](https://blog.olivierlarose.com/tutorials/blend-mode-cursor)). Forum fixes confirm that cursors block clicks without `pointer-events: none` ([Webflow forum](https://discourse.webflow.com/t/cursor-blending-mode-difference/172083)). The `pointer` and `hover` media queries test only the primary input, while `any-pointer` detects hybrids ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/pointer)). **Lenis** (v1.3.27) keeps native scroll, so `position: sticky`, IntersectionObserver and find-in-page still work. It forces lerp to 1 under reduced motion by default, caps at 60fps in Safari (30fps in Low Power Mode), stops smoothing over iframes, and needs `lenis/snap` instead of CSS scroll-snap ([Lenis](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis)). Locomotive Scroll v5 is now built on Lenis and disables parallax on touch ([Locomotive](https://scroll.locomotive.ca/docs/)). NN/g found scrolljacking disorients most users, is worst with text and on mobile, and confuses people when it turns horizontal ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolljacking-101/)). A preloader that is the only content on screen is exempt from WCAG 2.2.2, but parallel moving content over 5 s needs a pause control ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html)). Video players have consolidated. const mq = matchMedia('(hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)'), reduce = matchMedia('(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)');
const ring = document.querySelector('.cursor__ring'); let mx=0,my=0,rx=0,ry=0,on=false,raf;
const tick = () => { const t = reduce.matches ? 1 : 0.15; rx += (mx-rx)*t; ry += (my-ry)*t;
  ring.style.transform = `translate3d(${rx}px,${ry}px,0)`; raf = requestAnimationFrame(tick); };
const move = e => { if (e.pointerType !== 'mouse') return; mx=e.clientX; my=e.clientY;
  if (!on) { rx=mx; ry=my; on=true; ring.parentElement.classList.add('is-on'); } };
const enable = () => { addEventListener('pointermove', move, {passive:true}); raf = requestAnimationFrame(tick); };
if (mq.matches) enable();
mq.addEventListener('change', e => e.matches ? enable() : (cancelAnimationFrame(raf), removeEventListener('pointermove', move)));
// CSS: .cursor{position:fixed;pointer-events:none;z-index:9999} aria-hidden="true"; never cursor:none on body

## Animation: twelve families, CSS first, libraries only where they earn it

The platform now covers most motion natively, but support still varies by feature and needs checking. **Same-document View Transitions** are in Chrome 111, Safari 18 and Firefox 144. **Cross-document (MPA) transitions** (`@view-transition { navigation: auto }`) are in Chrome 126 and Safari 18.2, and Firefox is listed as partial on caniuse but "not supported" in Chrome's docs ([Chrome](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/view-transitions-in-2025); [caniuse](https://caniuse.com/cross-document-view-transitions)). Chrome skips a cross-document transition if navigation takes longer than 4 s ([Chrome](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/web-platform/view-transitions/cross-document)). **Scroll-driven animations** became Baseline Newly available on 27 October 2026. Watch three gotchas: the `animation` shorthand resets `animation-timeline`, so declare the timeline after it; Firefox needs a non-zero duration (use `1ms`); and an `overflow: hidden` ancestor silently retargets `scroll(nearest)`, which `overflow: clip` fixes ([DEV](https://dev.to/richicodes/scroll-driven-animations-are-baseline-at-last-3c8g)). Articles from earlier in 2026 that call Firefox "flag-only" are now outdated. "Newly available" also means many users have not yet updated, so keep fallbacks. `interpolate-size` (animating to `height: auto`) is still **Chromium-only** at about 74% globally ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/mdn-css_properties_interpolate-size)). Scroll-*triggered* animations (`timeline-trigger`, `animation-trigger`) are a separate Chromium-only feature from Chrome 145/146 ([Chrome](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/scroll-triggered-animations)). WebGPU now ships in Chrome/Edge, Safari 26 including iOS, and Firefox on Windows and Apple-silicon Macs ([TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/110456-webgpu-now-brings-desktop-quality-graphics-all-major.html)). three.js `WebGPURenderer` falls back to WebGL2 automatically, but it rejects `ShaderMaterial`, so new shader work belongs in TSL ([three.js](https://threejs.org/manual/en/webgpurenderer)). PixiJS v8 still recommends its WebGL renderer for production ([PixiJS](https://pixijs.com/8.x/guides/components/renderers)).

Library choice should follow need, and the default is no library. **Plain CSS transitions and WAAPI** cost 0 KB and run on the compositor for transform and opacity. Emil Kowalski notes that rAF-driven Motion animations can stutter when the main thread is busy ([Emil Kowalski](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/great-animations)). **Motion** (ex-Framer Motion) earns its place for `layout`/`layoutId` shared-element animation and `AnimatePresence` exits. Its LazyMotion + `m` setup is about 5 KB versus about 43 KB for the full component, per the docs' search snippets, though third-party numbers conflict ([motion.dev](https://motion.dev/docs/react-reduce-bundle-size)). **GSAP** is free, including SplitText (rewritten in 3.13 to be 50% smaller, with built-in masking and screen-reader attributes), MorphSVG, DrawSVG, Flip, ScrambleText, Inertia and ScrollSmoother ([GSAP 3.13](https://gsap.com/blog/3-13/)). It is the right tool for timelines, text splitting and pinned scroll scenes. **Anime.js v4** is modular at 24.5 KB total, with Draggable at 6.41 KB and Scroll at 4.30 KB ([Anime.js](https://animejs.com/documentation/getting-started/imports)). **AutoAnimate** gives one-line list add/remove/reorder and disables itself under reduced motion ([AutoAnimate](https://auto-animate.formkit.com/)). **Barba** (7 KB) and **Swup** still serve MPAs that need choreographed curtains or a persistent WebGL canvas ([Barba](https://barba.js.org/docs/getstarted/intro/); [Swup](https://swup.js.org/getting-started/)). Astro's `<ClientRouter />` adds `transition:persist` and disables itself under reduced motion, and Astro suggests checking whether native cross-document transitions are enough first ([Astro](https://docs.astro.build/en/guides/view-transitions/)). React 19.3's `<ViewTransition>` activates only through `startTransition`, `<Suspense>` or `useDeferredValue`, and **does not honour reduced motion automatically** ([react.dev](https://react.dev/reference/react/ViewTransition)). Next.js App Router supports view transitions with no configuration as of October 2026 ([Next.js](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/guides/view-transitions)). For vector assets, Rive's `@rive-app/webgl2` runtime needs `cleanup()` to free WASM objects ([Rive](https://rive.app/docs/runtimes/web/web-js)). dotLottie-web renders through Rust/WASM with Canvas2D, WebGL2 or WebGPU backends, embeds state machines, and can run on an OffscreenCanvas worker ([dotLottie](https://github.com/LottieFiles/dotlottie-web)).

## License-safe assets: default to CC0 and read every "free"

Asset licences fall into four tiers. **Tier A** is true CC0 or public domain, usable with no strings. **Tier B** uses custom "free" licences that allow commercial use without credit but ban standalone resale, redistribution to competing services, and often AI training or use in templates. **Tier C** sets licences per file, so you must check each asset. The last tier is attribution-required, or a trap: free tiers that require credit, are non-commercial, or are trial-only. Three rules prevent most mistakes. "Free for commercial use" does not mean CC0. A path licence (CC0 or MIT) never grants trademark rights to brand logos. Any theme or template that bundles stock images is a redistribution risk, so ship placeholders or CC0 assets in templates. The 2025–26 churn is heavy:

## Fast and fair on a mid-range phone, or it isn't finished

The performance targets have not changed: **LCP ≤ 2.5 s, INP ≤ 200 ms and CLS ≤ 0.1 at the 75th percentile**, measured separately on mobile and desktop. INP replaced FID in 2024 ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/vitals)). The Front-End Checklist adds a **1,500 KB page-weight** ceiling as a High-priority rule ([Front-End Checklist](https://github.com/thedaviddias/Front-End-Checklist)). Creative sites usually fail in three predictable ways. LCP fails when the hero is lazy-loaded or faded in from `opacity: 0` by JavaScript. INP fails when heavy pointer and scroll handlers or big bundles block the main thread. CLS fails from late fonts and unsized media. Animation cost is measurable: a web.dev test dropped **50% of frames animating top/left versus 1% with transform** ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/animations-guide)). Speculation Rules (Chromium) prerender likely next pages with `"eagerness": "moderate"` and are disabled under Save-Data and memory pressure ([Chrome](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/web-platform/prerender-pages)). Back/forward-cache restores come almost for free if you never use `unload`, avoid `Cache-Control: no-store` on HTML, and close connections in `pagehide` ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/bfcache)).

Fonts are the most common CLS source. Use **WOFF2 only** (about 30% smaller than WOFF), subset with `unicode-range`, inline `@font-face` in the head, preload sparingly, and use `font-display: optional` for body text and `swap` for brand faces ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/font-best-practices)). Match fallback metrics with Fontaine, whose playground cut **CLS from 0.24 to 0.054** ([Fontaine](https://github.com/unjs/fontaine)), or with Capsize for cap-height sizing ([Capsize](https://seek-oss.github.io/capsize/)). Prefer SVG to icon fonts, since fallback glyphs can change meaning. For icons, use named imports. Avoid `Icons[name]` lookups and barrel files, which keep every icon in the bundle, and switch to an external `<use href>` sprite on icon-dense screens ([Tabler](https://tabler.io/guides/how-to-use-svg-icons-in-react)). Serve AVIF with WebP and JPEG fallbacks, because AVIF alone risks broken images. JPEG XL is still unsupported in browsers ([web.dev](https://web.dev/learn/images/avif)). `sizes="auto"` for lazy images is now cross-browser with Safari 27 ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/web-platform-09-2026)). For video, list sources in preference order with `type` attributes, because the browser plays the first format it supports. Mobile Chrome **buffers nothing** ahead by default ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/video-and-source-tags)). Converting a 3.7 MB GIF produced a 551 KB MP4 or a 341 KB WebM ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/replace-gifs-with-videos)). Sound must be gesture-gated. Muted autoplay is always allowed, but an AudioContext created before a gesture starts suspended and must `resume()` after a click ([Chrome](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/autoplay)). use-sound adds under 1 kB plus about 9 kB of Howler, loaded asynchronously, and has a global `soundEnabled` mute ([use-sound](https://github.com/joshwcomeau/use-sound)). Ship 3D as GLB optimised with `gltf-transform optimize --compress draco --texture-compress webp`, or Meshopt and KTX2, after running `inspect` ([glTF Transform](https://gltf-transform.dev/cli)). Working budgets, which are practitioner heuristics rather than standards: cap DPR at 2 on desktop and 1.5 on mobile; keep textures at or below 2048px (1024 on mobile); keep hero models around 2–5 MB on mobile; keep motion JS around 60–80 KB gzip (GSAP core + ScrollTrigger + SplitText + Lenis); and lazy-load three.js only for the section that needs it.

Responsive work starts from content and intrinsic layout. Use fluid `clamp()` type and space with a `rem` term so zoom still works, `auto-fit/minmax` grids, and **container queries for components**. Keep media queries for macro layout and capabilities (`hover`, `pointer`, `prefers-*`). Viewport units matter on phones. Plain `vh` equals `lvh`, which is why `100vh` heroes get cut off under the URL bar. `svh` is the stable, "safer in general" choice, and `dvh` resizes during scroll ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/length)). iOS still has an open WebKit bug where scroll-snap with `100dvh` jumps sections ([WebKit](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=266835)). So use `svh` for pinned and sticky frames and keep `dvh` for static overlays. WCAG 2.2 AA requires **24×24 CSS px targets** (with a spacing exception) ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)), but 44–48px is the comfortable size for primary controls. Never disable pinch zoom ([Front-End Checklist](https://github.com/thedaviddias/Front-End-Checklist)).

```html
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, viewport-fit=cover">
<style>
.hero { min-block-size: 100svh; }                                   /* not 100vh */
.app-bar { padding-block-end: max(.75rem, env(safe-area-inset-bottom)); }
.btn, .nav a { min-block-size: 44px; min-inline-size: 44px; }       /* AA floor is 24px */
.cards { display:grid; gap:var(--space-s); grid-template-columns: repeat(auto-fit, minmax(min(18rem,100%),1fr)); }
.card-wrap { container-type: inline-size; }
@container (inline-size > 32rem) { .card { grid-template-columns: 2fr 3fr; } }
@media (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine) { .card:hover { translate: 0 -2px; } }   /* no sticky hover on touch */
</style>
<img src="hero.avif" width="1600" height="900" fetchpriority="high" alt="…">   <!-- LCP: never lazy, never JS-faded -->
```

For accessibility, build and test against **WCAG 2.2 AA**. WCAG 3 is a March 2026 Working Draft ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/news/2026-03-03/wcag3)), and APCA is **not** in it: the April 2026 editor's draft says the contrast algorithm is "yet to be determined", and WCAG 3 is "perhaps 2030 at the earliest" ([Adrian Roselli](https://adrianroselli.com/2026/04/wcag3-contrast-as-of-april-2026.html)). Meet the WCAG 2 contrast ratios (4.5:1 body, 3:1 large text and UI), use APCA only as extra guidance, and document any deviation. AI-generated components typically lack ARIA, keyboard support, validation and error states, so accessibility QA on generated code is mandatory ([prg.sh](https://prg.sh/ramblings/Why-Your-AI-Keeps-Building-the-Same-Purple-Gradient-Website)). Use native `<button>`, `<a href>`, `<dialog>`, `popover` and `<details>` before ARIA widgets. Give every component a designed `:focus-visible` state, keep sticky headers from obscuring focus, provide non-drag alternatives (2.5.7), and make sure the layout reflows at 320px.

On the stack, **Astro 7.4** (October 2026) suits content-led creative sites, because it ships zero JS by default and adds islands only where animation or interaction needs them ([Astro](https://astro.build/blog/)). Next.js, SvelteKit or Nuxt fit sites that are really apps, and Vite plus vanilla suits one-off WebGL pieces. **Tailwind v4** puts tokens in `@theme` as real CSS variables, uses an OKLCH palette, native cascade layers, built-in container queries and a `starting:` variant for `@starting-style` ([Tailwind](https://tailwindcss.com/blog/tailwindcss-v4)). Store tokens in the **DTCG format** (`$value`/`$type`, `.tokens.json`, latest published 2025.10) so design and code stay in sync ([designtokens.org](https://www.designtokens.org/tr/drafts/format/)). Utopia's method interpolates one type scale at a small viewport and another at a large one, so an H2 is always "step 4" without breakpoint overrides ([Utopia](https://utopia.fyi/blog/designing-with-fluid-type-scales/)).

## Conclusion

The center of gravity in web craft has moved. In 2023, a distinctive site depended on libraries and paid plugins: focus traps, Popper, Locomotive, Club GreenSock, JS masonry, custom select widgets. By November 2026 most of those jobs are either native, through `<dialog>`, Popover, invoker commands, anchor positioning, `@starting-style`, View Transitions and scroll-driven animation, or free, as GSAP now is. Technique has stopped being scarce. Judgement and editing are what remain scarce: which single interaction to keep, which effects to remove, and whether a curve has been tuned or left at its default. The libraries that made effects cheap also made them common. Shimmer buttons, border beams, bento glows and Lucide icons now mark a site as generic, and the "escape" fonts of 2024 already sit in the Google Fonts top 100. Distinctiveness has a half-life, so treat it as a moving target. A concept-driven system of type, colour, grid, copy and motion lasts longer than any trending effect.

Two risks are growing faster than most teams notice. The first is licensing. AI-training bans now appear in Unsplash+, Coverr, Sonniss, unDraw and DrawKit, Remix Icon's new licence forbids logo use, and several once-free sources have shut down or been absorbed into Freepik and Envato. Any site built with AI tooling, or shipped as a template, needs a licence log per asset. The second is device reality. Safari still caps animation at 60fps (30fps in Low Power Mode), mobile Chrome buffers no video ahead, and a single unsized hero or JS-revealed headline can undo every crafted detail. The practical conclusion is to design the reduced-motion, touch and low-GPU versions first, because those are what most real visitors will experience, and then add the signature moment as an enhancement.
